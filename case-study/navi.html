The Situation

Renewal existed.
It just didn't work.

At Navi, the app had a renewal flow. Almost nobody used it. Users were calling ops, using external portals, and delaying to the last moment.

That's not a UX problem — that's a business breakdown. Ops was absorbing the cost of what should have been fully self-serve.

The Problem

A failure for users.
A risk for the business.

Users were confused and anxious.

What changes at renewal? How close am I to lapsation? The experience gave no answers — so people delayed or called ops instead.

The business was absorbing the cost.

Late renewals and ops dependency meant rising lapsation risk, operational overhead, and no path to scaling renewals as a reliable self-serve product.

"Users must renew on time to keep their policy active — but the experience was fragmented and unclear, leading to delayed renewals, ops reliance, and rising lapsation risk."

The real job-to-be-done

"The job wasn't 'renew'.
It was: help me avoid lapsation
confidently, with minimum effort."

Exploration

Four directions.
One winner.

Rather than optimising the existing flow, I explored four strategic directions before converging on one scalable solution.

Variant 01

Information-first approach

Explored because: users said they lacked context before renewing.
Moved past: too many screens increased drop-off before the key action.

Variant 02

Progressive disclosure flow

Explored because: revealed details step-by-step to reduce anxiety.
Moved past: added cognitive load without improving completion rate.

Variant 03

Nudge-forward approach

Explored because: behavioural prompts could drive earlier renewals.
Moved past: the core flow wasn't reliable enough to layer nudges on top.

Selected ✓
Variant 04

Clarity + confidence approach

Why it won: clearest renewal status, minimal steps, strongest "I can do this myself" confidence, and highest engineering feasibility for rollout speed.

Why V4 won: Optimised for low cognitive load and fast completion — targeted communication covers trust gaps. Simplicity moves people. Complexity stalls them.

Key Decisions

What we chose.
And why.

01

Why simplify instead of adding more reassurance UI?

More screens = more drop-off. Contextual hints and communication cover trust gaps more effectively than additional interface.

02

Why fix the core flow before adding nudges?

Nudges don't fix a broken flow — we shipped reliability first, then planned behaviour nudges from real post-launch data.

03

Why Variant 4 over the other three?

Best balance of renewal clarity, minimal steps, self-serve confidence, and engineering feasibility.

04

What did we intentionally leave out of V1?

Last-minute renewers — we shipped the foundational system first and let real data define the next lever.

What I Learned

Ship the system.
Then optimise behaviour.

Insight 01

Renewals are trust moments, not just payment steps. The experience has to feel safe before users will complete it on their own.

Insight 02

The fastest way to reduce lapsation is clarity and completion inside the app — not more UI, more screens, or more reassurance copy.

Insight 03

Real data from a shipped product is worth more than assumptions from research. Ship the foundation. Let the data tell you what to build next.
